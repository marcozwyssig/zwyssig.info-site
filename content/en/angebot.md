### Software Architecture

Developing software is a complex undertaking, and software architecture is what structures the software into components and defines how these components interact. Success depends on many factors, and errors can creep in at any phase that are difficult to correct afterwards.

We advise on, review and evaluate architectures, tools, designs and source code against your product's defined non-functional quality goals and against object-oriented design principles (OOD). Together with the development team, we deliver concrete proposals for how you can improve your product in order to reduce potential productivity losses and quality shortcomings, thereby containing project risk.

### Requirements Analysis

We support you in turning an idea into a software solution that does the right things, the right way. Concrete, measurable goals and requirements (epics, user stories) are derived and structured from the product vision. In doing so, we focus on the principle of return on investment (ROI). Together with the customer, we prioritize the themes (epics) by their value in order to achieve the highest possible effectiveness, so that they are also correctly and completely defined. We capture the elicited requirements in the backlog or in a specification and store them in a version control system, so that changes remain traceable at all times (change management).

We use a variety of techniques to elicit the requirements needed for the future software solution (requirements elicitation):

- Joint brainstorming
- Change of perspective
- Field observation
- "Job shadowing"
- Questionnaire
- Interview
- Self-reporting
- On-site customer
- System archaeology
- Reuse

### Pair Programming

Pair Programming is an agile development method that was first established within Extreme Programming and is now also used when applying other agile frameworks. The concept of Pair Programming has two developers sit down together and work jointly on a task, taking turns at the keyboard. Especially for central and complex topics, we work with your .NET development team using Pair Programming, so that you achieve the following improvements in your .NET development team and product:

- Higher quality through fewer defects
- Higher productivity by avoiding knowledge silos, and lower additional costs from defects discovered late
- Higher stability, extensibility and testability
